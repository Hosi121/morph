## Original equations, shape provenance, and both ideal directions

Let `I` be the ideal generated by the raw certificate's supplied rows and `J=<g(t),x_i-h_i(t)>`. The selected eliminant and each selected coordinate row must be actual members of those rows. Their normalization uses only nonzero rational scalars and reduction by `g`, establishing `J ⊆ I`. Substitution of **every** supplied row modulo `g` establishes `I ⊆ J`. These checks therefore establish equality of ideals, not just a one-way map from parameters into some proposed equations.

This argument does not invoke the shape lemma to assume that an arbitrary zero-dimensional problem has the required form. The classical lemma includes radicality and a separating-coordinate hypothesis; see [Szántó, Geometric Resolution, Theorem 1.1](https://aszanto.math.ncsu.edu/MA722/ln-03.pdf). Here the actual presentation is checked, and repeated eliminants remain valid nonradical presentations.

The F4 wrapper first verifies the saved original input equations and the F4 certificate, then requires the raw proof's variable count and row list to equal that verified basis. The underlying F4 checker reconstructs ideal membership witnesses and checks reduction of all original inputs, as well as Gröbner-basis conditions. A valid shape proof for a changed raw equation list is consequently not a proof for the previous verified basis.

The adversarial tests exercise both directions separately: missing selected rows, a forged eliminant with no source row, an actual smaller factor added as a new equation, an actual larger eliminant while retaining an incompatible original row, an extra coordinate constraint, an altered coordinate expression, and a redundant consequence. A smaller factor plus its genuine source and adjusted coordinates is accepted for the changed raw problem; the old-basis wrapper rejects it. A redundant added consequence is likewise a valid raw presentation but differs from the exact verified basis list. Reusing the F4 certificate against changed original input equations is rejected.

A nonzero constant actual row certifies the unit ideal. Two different inconsistent original equation lists may independently verify to the same reduced unit basis `[1]`; a unit proof is correctly reusable between those verified bases. It is not a cryptographic assertion that the original textual input lists are identical. A nonunit basis, absent source constant, zero witness, and foreign constant not present in the row list are rejected.

## A different oracle from the existing CAS fixtures

[The generator](../../tools/generate_realvariety_review.py) uses only Python's standard-library `Fraction` and elementary polynomial operations. It never calls SymPy, a resultant, a determinant, a Gröbner-basis implementation, or a production executable. Starting from explicitly factored `g=∏(t-r)^m` and coordinate polynomials, it independently computes each expected full norm as

```text
C_h(z) = ∏ (z-h(r))^m.
```

It also independently reduces coordinate expressions and evaluates every original equation at each expected rational tuple. Original input rows are obtained by an invertible upper-triangular polynomial row map with nonzero rational diagonal. Thus their ideal equality to the initial shape presentation is constructive. Negative rational scales and nontrivial polynomial row combinations exercise normalization and F4 reconstruction. In the three-variable cases the raw mixed rows are deliberately unsupported, and verified F4 supplies the shape presentation.

The seven systems have 17 distinct rational parameter roots and 42 point-coordinate evaluations. They cover two or three variables, eliminant degree two through four, multiplicities up to four, fractions with denominators 7, 9, 11, 13, 17, and 31, a repeated zero, nearby roots `0,1/31,2/31`, coordinate collisions, reversed coordinate order, zero and nonzero constant coordinates, and coordinate inputs of degree nine reduced modulo a repeated quartic. Large rational numerators and denominators use arbitrary binary magnitudes in generated tests. The retained oracle is `.tools/realvariety-review-20261116/fraction-oracle.json`.

For every returned parameter interval, direct rational inequalities check that it contains exactly one of the complete known roots under the convention `(lower,upper]`. Every materialized coordinate interval contains its independently known rational value, and comparison against that rational value agrees. Each full norm polynomial is compared coefficient-for-coefficient with the independent product, including multiplicities.

## Root covers, norm interpolation, and returned payloads

Complete covers must contain all distinct real parameter roots exactly once. Missing, repeated, reversed, excessive, and foreign intervals are rejected. Touching intervals `(-1,0],(0,1],(1,2]` are accepted: each root belongs to one interval, and this RUR enumerator has no open-cell sample obligation requiring a positive gap. A valid real-algebraic number for a root of a different polynomial cannot serve as a parameter for this shape.

For `d=degree(g)`, multiplication by `h` in `Q[t]/(g)` has a `d*d` matrix. For any root `α` of `g`, evaluating its columns gives a nonzero left eigenvector with eigenvalue `h(α)`, so `h(α)` is a root of `det(zI-M_h)`. This reasoning supports repeated `g` as well. The multiplication-map/root-value relation is also stated in [Verschelde, Rational Univariate Representation, Stickelberger's theorem](https://homepages.math.uic.edu/~jan/mcs563s14/lec12.pdf).

The checker requires a claimed polynomial of degree at most `d`, exactly `d+1` samples at distinct rational points `0,...,d`, and independently recomputed determinant values at those points. Equality at these points uniquely identifies that bounded-degree polynomial. The tests add `z(z-1)(z-2)(z-3)` to a degree-three norm: all four samples agree, but the degree cap rejects the forgery. Missing, excess, and altered samples are rejected. For the nonradical system `x=0,t^4=0`, the full norm is `z^4`; replacing it by `z` is rejected even though the selected Number and its zero set are unchanged.

The refined source interval is checked inside the original parameter interval and contains its unique selected source root. Closed Horner evaluation encloses `h(α)`; adding a positive source-width margin to both ends keeps endpoint values and extrema strictly inside the resulting half-open interval. Requiring one root of the square-free norm there selects the correct conjugate. The tests reject a valid wrong conjugate, a foreign source refinement, an altered embedded point or index, and a normal proof paired with another returned Number.

`check_point_result`, `check_solutions_result`, and `check_coordinate_result` additionally bind a supplied payload to the certificate's exact representation. An equivalent root encoded by a different interval is deliberately not interchangeable with that representation. A standalone coordinate certificate certifies its **embedded** point and index: an application validating a separately requested query must also compare those embedded fields with the expected query. Likewise, raw proofs certify their embedded equation list; original-input binding belongs to the verified-basis wrapper.

## Fuel, limits, and reproducibility

A dedicated degree-three example needs four determinant/interpolation steps and one source refinement. Budgets zero through four remain paused; zero additional fuel does not complete them. Resuming with exactly the missing fuel produces the same certificate and Number as a five-step run, and budgets five, six, and twenty complete identically. Constructor work, checking, arithmetic within a step, and source root-count computations are outside this step-count budget. Existing tests cover root-isolation resumability; this review adds the coordinate transition boundary.

The fixture harness imposes 300 F4 batches, 2048 root-isolation nodes, and 2048 coordinate sample/refinement steps, failing explicitly if these bounds are exceeded. This is a finite review of small rational-root shape systems and selected malicious certificates. It does not exhaust rational coefficients, prove arbitrary F4 termination, audit a future separating-form search, or formally verify the entire checker. Polynomial normal forms, exact rational arithmetic, F4 certificate checking, determinant calculation, Sturm root counts, and real-algebraic certificate checking remain shared dependencies. The independent oracle supplies different information but does not remove those trust boundaries.
