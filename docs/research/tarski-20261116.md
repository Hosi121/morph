# Exact Sturm–Tarski sign queries, 2026-11-16

This package computes the integer sum of `sign(Q(r))` over the **distinct** real roots of a nonzero rational univariate polynomial `P` in the finite half-open interval `(a,b]`. It also determines the sign of `Q(alpha)` at an existing checked one-root representation, without root isolation, interval refinement, floating point, or algebraic-number arithmetic.

[Producer](../../src/tarski/query.mbt), [certificate checker](../../src/tarski/check.mbt), [algebraic root sign API](../../src/tarski/root_sign.mbt), [compiler-generated API](../../src/tarski/pkg.generated.mbti), [independent generator](../../tools/generate_tarski_fixtures.py).

The first complete milestone passes **402 package tests on native, JS and WasmGC**. Existing `sturm`, `polynomial`, `realalgebraic`, and earlier `realvariety` code/artifacts are unchanged. This is exact executable code with certificate checking; it is not a formal proof of the MoonBit implementation, an optimal complexity claim, or a benchmark comparison.

## Domain and outputs

Both input polynomials must declare exactly one variable, including zero/constant polynomials. `P=0` is rejected because its real root set is not finite. A nonzero constant `P` yields zero, any zero `Q` yields zero, common roots contribute zero, and repeated roots are counted once. Bounds are rational and must satisfy `a<b`; equal/reversed bounds are rejected. Infinite endpoints are outside this API.

`query(P,Q,interval)` returns a signed exact `Integer` plus a `QueryCertificate`. `check_query(P,Q,interval,certificate)` binds the separately supplied original polynomials and interval before verifying the claim. `check_query_result` additionally binds the separately supplied integer payload. A zero query can mean cancellation; it does not mean that there are no roots or that `Q` vanishes at every root.

`root_sign(RootCertificate,Q)` first validates the existing half-open one-root certificate, then runs the query on that same polynomial and interval. The integer must be one of `-1,0,1`; its returned `Ordering` means `Less=negative`, `Equal=zero`, `Greater=positive`. `check_root_sign` binds the selected root definition, exact interval, query polynomial, ordering payload, and complete query proof. `sign_at`/`check_sign_at` provide the same interface for an opaque `@realalgebraic.Number`. Valid equivalent numbers with different root representations are not interchangeable in these exact representation checks.

## Source theorem and endpoint convention

The starting result is the open-interval Sturm–Tarski theorem for endpoints that are not roots of `P`: the signed remainder sequence of `P,P'*Q` computes the Tarski query by a sign-variation difference. [Basu–Pollack–Roy, *Algorithms in Real Algebraic Geometry*, Proposition 2.57 and Theorems 2.58/2.61](https://perso.univ-rennes1.fr/marie-francoise.roy/bpr-ed2-posted2.pdf) relates the query to the Cauchy index of `P'*Q/P` and then to signed remainders. [Li–Paulson, §5.1, equations (35)–(37)](https://wenda302.github.io/assets/pdf/cauchy_jar.pdf) states the same endpoint restriction and distinguishes classical and endpoint-adjusted index conventions. The [AFP Sturm–Tarski development](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Sturm_Tarski.html) is a primary formal reference for that theorem; this package does not claim to import its proof.

For the required `(a,b]` convention we derive the following extension rather than silently applying the open-interval formula at roots. Let `S` be the signed remainder sequence, and let `V_+(x)` be its sign-variation count using each entry's **right-hand limit** sign. Then

```text
TaQ(Q,P;(a,b]) = V_+(a) - V_+(b).
```

To justify this, choose a sufficiently small positive epsilon so that no entry of the finite nonzero sequence has another root immediately to the right of either endpoint. Its signs at `a+epsilon,b+epsilon` are exactly the right-hand signs. These shifted points satisfy the source theorem's non-root hypothesis. The roots in the open shifted interval `(a+epsilon,b+epsilon)` are exactly the roots in `(a,b]`: an endpoint root at `a` is excluded and one at `b` is included. Applying the open theorem and taking these stable signs gives the displayed formula. This endpoint extension is our stated derivation, independently tested below; it is not presented as a verbatim endpoint theorem from the cited sources.

Ordinary zero-skipping at the original endpoints is insufficient. For `P=x,Q=-1,(-1,0]`, the sequence is `[x,-1]`. Dropping the zero at the upper endpoint gives variation difference zero, while the correct query is `-1`. Right-hand signs at zero are `[+,-]`, giving `0-1=-1`. The same issue occurs with repeated `P=x^2` and a negative query at its upper endpoint.

## Signed remainders, multiplicities, and witnesses

The producer normalizes only the initial polynomial to monic `F=P/lc(P)`, retaining all repeated factors. It forms `B=rem(F'*Q,F)` and the exact rational sequence

```text
S_0=F, S_1=B,
S_(i+1) = -rem(S_(i-1),S_i),
```

stopping before a zero entry. If `B=0`, the sequence is the singleton `[F]`. Remainders and the seed are **not** independently made monic: such a normalization could reverse essential signs. Replacing `F'*Q` by its remainder only subtracts a polynomial from the rational function and leaves all finite pole jumps unchanged.

No square-free reduction is needed. At a root `r` of multiplicity `m>0`, `F'/F` has the local simple-pole term `m/(x-r)`. Multiplication by `Q` gives a contribution with the sign of `m*Q(r)`, hence of `Q(r)`, once per distinct root; when `Q(r)=0` the pole cancels. Common factors may remain in every sequence entry and in its final gcd. Their common sign multiplication does not alter a variation count. The implementation never assumes the final gcd is a nonzero constant.

The certificate records every quotient, with identities `S_(i-1)=q_i*S_i-S_(i+1)` and strict degree descent. The last quotient must prove the exact terminal divisibility `S_(k-1)=q_k*S_k`. The checker recomputes the initial normalized polynomial and reduced seed, then verifies those polynomial identities and degrees instead of regenerating the Euclidean chain. Truncated chains, missing terminal quotients, changed remainder signs, and mismatched sequences are rejected.

For each nonzero sequence entry at each endpoint, a `RightJet` gives the least derivative order `k` with nonzero value `S^(k)(a)`. Taylor's formula makes its sign the right-hand limit sign because `k!` and the small positive displacement power are positive. The checker verifies all earlier derivatives vanish, verifies the exact nonzero recorded value, and requires `k<=degree(S)`. An arbitrary nonzero higher derivative is not enough. These signs produce the two natural-number variation counts; subtraction uses the symbolic signed integer type, preserving negative queries.

Both the PRS and derivative witnesses terminate by degree bounds. There is no search fuel or interval-refinement loop here; exact rational coefficient growth, recursion depth, and runtime/memory are practical limits, and no global bit-operation or elapsed-time bound is claimed. Production source contains no call to `refine`, `advance`, or `isolate`. Root-certificate validation uses the existing exact one-root checker but never changes its interval.

## Independent validation

The [fixture artifact](tarski-20261116-fixtures.json) contains **356 queries**: 130 negative, 123 zero and 103 positive. It includes exact rational-root families with known multiplicities and nonreal extra factors, every monic degree-two/three polynomial with lower coefficients in `{-1,0,1}` against four queries and two intervals, and repeated/common-root algebraic mixtures. Semantic expected sums come directly from known rational roots using Python `Fraction`, or from SymPy 1.14.0 exact real roots and exact signs; they do not use the PRS formula to obtain the expected sum. A separate dense `Fraction` Euclidean implementation supplies the expected full sequence.

A further finite-difference oracle checks every endpoint right sign independently of symbolic differentiation. It translates each sequence polynomial exactly as `R(a+u)=sum(c_j*u^j)`. If `c_k` is its first nonzero coefficient and `C=sum(j>k,abs(c_j))`, it chooses a common positive rational epsilon bounded by `1/2`, `(b-a)/4`, and `abs(c_k)/(2*C)` when `C>0`. Then the tail after factoring `u^k` is at most half the first coefficient in magnitude. Evaluating directly at `a+epsilon` and `b+epsilon` therefore certifies the true right signs, even at common/repeated endpoint roots. MoonBit agrees with every independently generated sign and semantic query.

The **40 algebraic-root sign fixtures** include positive/negative square roots, a cube root, `sqrt(2)+sqrt(3)` as a quartic root, repeated and reducible definitions, exact endpoint roots, common query roots, close roots around one, and the rational thresholds immediately below/above `sqrt(2)` at scale `2^40`. Every result retains exactly the supplied interval. Separate adversarial tests reject forged integer/order payloads, other valid conjugates, changed external P/Q/bounds, false jet orders or values, missing entries, altered quotient identities, and unfinished chains. A cancelling multi-root query is explicitly rejected as an input to the one-root sign API. Query additivity across a root and sign-count identities provide additional exact checks.
